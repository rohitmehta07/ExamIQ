Chapter 3: Advanced Encryption Standard (AES) Architecture.

Advanced Encryption Standard (AES) is a symmetric block cipher standardized by NIST in FIPS 197.
AES processes data blocks of 128 bits using secret key sizes of 128, 192, or 256 bits.
Each AES round consists of four distinct transformations: SubBytes, ShiftRows, MixColumns, and AddRoundKey.
SubBytes is an invertible non-linear byte substitution utilizing an 8x8 S-box in Galois Field GF(2^8).
MixColumns operates on state columns treating each column as a four-term polynomial modulo x^4 + 1.
Active attacks differ from passive attacks in that active attacks alter the communication stream whereas passive attacks monitor traffic without alteration.
The Avalanche Effect is a fundamental cryptographic property where a single bit change in plaintext induces changes in over 50 percent of ciphertext bits.
